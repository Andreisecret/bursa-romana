\begin{abstract}
We study how quickly and how strongly the Bucharest Stock Exchange reacted to
\NEvents{} political events from 2020 to 2026. In the three elections we examine
at 15-minute intervals, much of the price change was already visible at the next
market open. Over three trading sessions, the average absolute abnormal return
was $\EvMeanAbs\%$ around political events and $\NullMeanAbs\%$ in the comparison
sample ($p=\AggP$). Three events passed the 5\% threshold individually. Political
drops recovered within 60 sessions, as did most ordinary drops of similar size.
The best trading rule earned $\BestGross\%$ per trade before costs and
$\BestNetFifty\%$ after a cost of 50 basis points.
\end{abstract}

\section{Data and method}

We downloaded daily prices in RON from the BVB data feed
(\url{https://wapi.bvb.ro/api}) from 1 January 2020 through 28 September 2026.
The sample includes BET, four other indices (ROTX, BET-TR, BET-FI and BET-NG),
and eight stocks (TLV, SNP, BRD, H2O, SNG, DIGI, TEL and SNN). H2O starts at its
July 2023 listing. We also collected 15-minute prices for BET and TLV around
three presidential election rounds. For the European benchmark, we used the
STOXX Europe 600 series from Yahoo Finance and carried the last available close
forward when a BVB session had no European quote.

We use BET-TR as the main series because it includes dividends. The feed's
\texttt{ajust} option gave the same $-4.69\%$ TLV return on 11 June 2024 with or
without adjustment. We flag any event where the BET and BET-TR results differ
by more than one percentage point over the same window.

Table~\ref{tab:events} lists the \NEvents{} manually dated events, their
classification and our confidence in the dates. We also collect recent
political headlines from G4Media, HotNews, Digi24 and Economica by RSS.
We assign news from a trading day before 18:00 to that session and other news
to the next session. For the Sunday election rounds, day 0 is Monday.

Following the event-study approach in \cite{mackinlay1997}, we measure an
abnormal return as the actual return minus an estimate of the usual return.
For the indices we use the average return from sessions $-60$ through $-11$,
with at least 30 observations. For individual stocks we fit a linear model
using BET. We also fit BET against STOXX 600 as a check on European market
movements. The cumulative abnormal return (CAR) adds abnormal returns over
day 0, sessions $[-1,+1]$ or sessions $[-5,+5]$. The conventional test uses
$t=CAR/(\hat{\sigma}\sqrt{T})$, where $T$ is the number of sessions in the
event window.

A calm estimation period can make this test overstate how unusual a move is.
We therefore apply the same calculation to \NNull{} randomly selected trading
sessions, excluding the three sessions on either side of each recorded event.
These are our placebo dates. For each event, we compare the size of its CAR
with the placebo distribution. We also compare average absolute returns across
the two groups and repeat the exercise with different estimation windows.

\subsection{How unusual were these moves?}

In the placebo sample, the three-session CAR had a mean of $\NullMean\%$
and a standard deviation of $\NullSd\%$. The 95th percentile for its absolute
value was $\NullPqfive\%$, and the largest move was $\NullMax\%$. A move of
at least 3\% occurred in $\NullFreqThree\%$ of placebo windows.

Three of the \NTested{} testable events passed the 5\% threshold. The May 2025
runoff had a CAR of $\TurTwoCar\%$ ($p=\TurTwoP$), the court annulment
$\CcrCar\%$ ($p=\CcrP$), and the Fitch outlook revision $\FitchCar\%$

of emergency produced the largest move in the sample and had too little prior

threshold by chance. Across the \AggN{} distinct event sessions, average
absolute CAR was $\EvMeanAbs\%$, compared with $\NullMeanAbs\%$ in the
placebo sample ($p=\AggP$, based on \AggPerm{} random draws).

\subsection{Estimation windows and event groups}

The sensitivity check uses four estimation windows of 40 to 250 sessions.
Only \NSens{} events have enough data for the longest window. The smallest
rank correlation was $\RhoWindow$ and the number of events below $p=0.05$
ranged from \NPfiveWshort{} to \NPfiveWlong{}. The May runoff, the annulment
and the Fitch revision pass in all four checks. The 95th percentile ranges
from $\PqWshort\%$ for the shortest window to $\PqWlong\%$ for the longest.
The longest window also excludes the 2020 crash because it needs more prior
data. In a separate post-COVID check, the percentile was $\PqPostCovid\%$,
close to the baseline result.

\section{Trading after the news}

The opening gap accounted for \GapShareLo{}--\GapShareHi\% of the three-session
move. The remaining move ranged from $\AfterNewsMin\%$ to $\AfterNewsMax\%$
in absolute terms. Capturing the gap requires a position before the first
post-news session.

We split the sample chronologically into \ReguliTrain{} training events and
\ReguliTest{} test events. Of the \NRules{} candidate rules, \NTradable{}
take a position. The highest average gross return came from going short at
the open on every event day. It earned $\BestGross\%$ per trade before costs
and $\BestNetFifty\%$ at a round-trip cost of 50 basis points. The estimated
break-even cost was \BreakevenBps{} basis points. We varied costs because we
did not measure the actual spread, commission or price impact.

The comparison with the best rule in each placebo draw gives $p=\RealityP$.
The highest observed $t$-statistic was already negative at 50 basis points.
In an earlier calculation the cost on a short trade had the wrong sign.
Charging the same cost for long and short trades removed the apparent gain
at 50 basis points.

With \NPower{} events and a standard deviation of $\SdTrade\%$ per trade,
the standard error is $\SeTrade\%$. The estimated effect needed for 50\%
power at the 5\% level is $\Mde\%$ per trade. Detecting the best observed
gross return with 80\% power would take about \NNeed{} events. The difference
between entry at the open and entry one session earlier suggests a pre-event
rise of about 0.2\%, which we leave open given the sample size.

\section{Three events that need extra context}\label{sec:masurari}

\paragraph{The Ciucă government.}
The November 2021 investiture window produced a CAR of $\CiucaCar\%$
($t=\CiucaT$). BET rose $\CiucaInvestDay\%$ on the investiture day.
The next day, during the Omicron selloff, BET fell $\CiucaBet\%$ and STOXX
600 fell $\CiucaStoxx\%$. The model estimated a European-market coefficient
of $\CiucaBetaEst$ before the event and $\CiucaBetaPost$ afterwards. The low
pre-event estimate explains why the model showed a domestic shortfall of
2.04\% even though BET fell less than STOXX 600 that day.

\paragraph{The June 2024 elections.}
BET shows a CAR of $\LocaleBet\%$ ($t=\LocaleT$), compared with $\LocaleTr\%$
for BET-TR. Several bank shares went ex-dividend in the same window. This
was the only event flagged by the BET versus BET-TR check. We excluded it
from the group averages and kept it in the individual and aggregate tests.

\paragraph{The court annulment.}
On 6 December 2024, BET-TR posted an abnormal return of $+\CcrArZero\%$ and
a three-session CAR of $\CcrCar\%$. Two sessions earlier BET had fallen
3.24\% intraday before closing positive. The estimation window ends before
the late-November decline, and the rebound already under way makes it hard
to isolate the effect of the decision.

\section{Limitations}

The sample is small and manually dated. Two events have medium confidence.
BET changed composition over time, while our stock list is fixed. Individual
stock returns can still include dividend effects. The intraday results cover
three heavily traded sessions and use 15-minute closes, which can miss highs
and lows within each interval.

The feed reproduces the reported BET close of 16,982 points and the 0.89\%
decline on 25 November 2024 \cite{bursa}. It also gives a 0.45\% decline on
the second session after the first round in May 2025, close to the reported
half-percent drop \cite{profit}. We could not independently match the
reported intraday low from that coverage.

\section{Conclusion}

The largest election reactions were already visible at the next open.
Across the full sample, political event windows had larger price moves on
average than placebo windows. The recovery comparison and backtest give
little support for trading after the news at the tested costs.

The repository includes the data, code and a monitor that logs new events
before their recovery is known. A larger set of events recorded this way
would help test whether the small patterns in this sample recur.
