\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Question.} How fast, and how strongly, do shares on the Bucharest Stock
Exchange react to political news? We apply an event study to \NEvents{} events over
2020--2026, using daily quotes for the BET index, 8 liquid stocks and 4 complementary
indices, plus 15-minute bars around the three major electoral shocks.

\noindent\textbf{Speed.} The response forms at the open of the first session after the event.
In November 2024 the lowest 15-minute bar came 15 minutes after the open.

\noindent\textbf{Magnitude, in two senses.} Calibrated with a placebo test of \NNull{}
pseudo-events run through an identical estimator, the null distribution of three-day cumulative
abnormal returns has $\sigma = \NullSd\%$ and a 95th percentile of $\NullPqfive\%$, so a 3\%
move over three days occurs in roughly $\NullFreqThree\%$ of windows drawn from sessions with
no political news at all. Read one event at a time, three of the \NTested{} testable events
clear the 5\% threshold, the second-round presidential victory being the clearest. Read in
aggregate, political events do depart from ordinary noise: mean absolute movement is
$\EvMeanAbs\%$ on event days against $\NullMeanAbs\%$ on ordinary days, $p = \AggP$ in a
permutation test.

\noindent\textbf{Robustness.} Re-running the placebo test with four estimation windows, from
40 to 250 sessions, leaves the conclusion unchanged but not the details: the lowest rank
correlation across windows is $\RhoWindow$ and the count of events at $p < 0.05$ moves between
$\NPfiveWshort$ and $\NPfiveWlong$. Restricting the null to the post-COVID regime gives a 95th
percentile of $\PqPostCovid\%$, essentially the full-period value, so the fat tails are not an
artifact of the pandemic.

\noindent\textbf{Recovery and tradability.} All \RecPolRecovered\% of political drops of at
least 1\% return to the pre-shock close within 60 sessions, and so do \RecPlRecovered\% of
random drops of matched depth, a median $\RecMedDiff$ sessions \emph{faster} on the control
side and not significantly ($\RecP$, $\RecPairN$ pairs). The opening gap absorbs
$\GapShareLo$--$\GapShareHi$\% of the three-day move, and the best of \NRules{} pre-declared
rules earns $\BestGross\%$ gross but $\BestNetFifty$\% net at 50 basis points, with breakeven
at $\BreakevenBps$ basis points.

\noindent\emph{Methodological contribution.} Three of the most eye-catching results of an
ordinary event study turn out to be measurement failures rather than market phenomena. A fixed
estimation window that lands in a calm regime inflates every $t$-statistic at once. The public
BVB feed's adjustment flag does not correct for dividends, so ex-dividend windows are
contaminated. A market control estimated in calm conditions can turn a global crash day into a
fictitious domestic effect.
\end{abstract}

\section{Data and method}

\subsection{Quotes}

The Bucharest Stock Exchange publishes no developer API, but its website runs a
TradingView-compatible datafeed at \url{https://wapi.bvb.ro/api} that answers plain HTTP
requests. The parameters are \texttt{symbol}, \texttt{from}, \texttt{to}, \texttt{rs},
\texttt{ajust}, \texttt{countback} and \texttt{currencyCode}, and the daily resolution must be
requested as \texttt{rs=1D}. We downloaded daily bars in RON for 1 January 2020 to
28 September 2026: the BET index, 8 liquid stocks (TLV, SNP, BRD, H2O, SNG, DIGI, TEL, SNN)
and 4 complementary indices (ROTX, BET-TR, BET-FI, BET-NG), about 1,690 sessions per symbol.
H2O enters the series only in July 2023, with the largest IPO in the exchange's history. For
the speed analysis we also downloaded 15-minute bars for BET and TLV around the three major
shocks.

One limitation of the source changes the results, so we state it here. The \texttt{ajust} flag
corrects stock splits but not dividends, and the check is direct: TLV's return on 11 June
2024, $-4.69\%$, is identical in the adjusted and unadjusted series. Price returns are
therefore contaminated in ex-dividend windows, and the Romanian banks' dividend season, in
June, falls in the middle of the window we analyse. The primary series is accordingly
\textbf{BET-TR}, the exchange's total-return index, which is immune to this effect. In
parallel, the pipeline flags any event where the gap between BET and BET-TR exceeds one
percentage point over the same window.

\subsection{Benchmark and events}

As a model of the European market we used STOXX Europe 600, downloaded daily from Yahoo
Finance's public chart interface and aligned to the exchange's calendar by forward-filling the
last available value.

Articles from the RSS feeds of G4Media, HotNews, Digi24 and Economica were collected
automatically and keyword-filtered for political content. RSS covers only the present, so the
historical base is a table of \NEvents{} manually dated events, each with a confidence level
and an \emph{ex-ante} classification of origin (domestic, external, market) and expected
direction. Table~\ref{tab:events} lists them.

\subsection{Estimator}

The estimation window is $[-60,-11]$ trading days, truncated at the start of the series, with
a minimum of 30 observations. Event windows are $[0,0]$, $[-1,+1]$ and $[-5,+5]$. For BET and
the indices the abnormal return is $AR_t = R_t - \bar{R}_{est}$. For the individual stocks we
use an OLS market model against BET, and for the external control an OLS model of BET against
STOXX 600, both estimated over the estimation window. The conventional statistic is
$t = CAR / (\hat{\sigma}\sqrt{T})$.

Day 0 is the session in which the news appears if that falls before 18:00, and the next
trading session otherwise. Every ballot was held on a Sunday evening, so day 0 is the Monday
and the opening price carries the full weekend information.

\subsection{Placebo calibration}

The $t$-statistics above assume normally distributed, independent returns with volatility
measured over the estimation window. On a small and illiquid market that assumption is
problematic: a fixed window of 50 sessions can land in a calm regime and understate the
volatility used as the denominator. To remove the dependence on a parameter we chose
ourselves, we calibrate with a placebo test. We draw 500 pseudo-random trading sessions,
excluding three sessions either side of every real event, and apply the identical estimator.
The resulting distribution shows how large an ordinary CAR is at BVB, and each real event
receives a two-sided empirical $p$-value on $|CAR|$.

Two further tests follow from the same null. The aggregate test asks whether mean absolute
movement is larger on event days than on ordinary days, which sidesteps the multiple-comparisons
problem of examining \NTested{} events individually. The window-sensitivity test re-runs the whole
calibration under four different estimation windows, which addresses the paper's own objection
that a fixed window may be unrepresentative.

\subsection{Calibrating the magnitude}

The placebo test recalibrates the reading of everything else. The null distribution of
CAR$[-1,+1]$ has a mean of $\NullMean\%$ and $\sigma = \NullSd\%$, with $|CAR_3|$ at the 95th
percentile of $\NullPqfive\%$ and a maximum of $\NullMax\%$. A 3\% move over three sessions
occurs in $\NullFreqThree\%$ of the $\NNull$ pseudo-event windows drawn from sessions with no
political news at all, so the effective detectability threshold for a political event is of
order 4\%.

\centering

\label{tab:placebo}

Of the \NTested{} of \NEvents{} political events that have a full estimation window, three clear
the 5\% threshold: the second-round presidential victory in May 2025 at $\TurTwoCar\%$ and
$p = \TurTwoP$ (percentile $\TurTwoPct$), the Constitutional Court's annulment of the election
at $\CcrCar\%$ and $p = \CcrP$, and the Fitch outlook revision at $-\FitchCar\%$ and
$p = \FitchP$. The December 2024 parliamentary elections sit at $p = \ParlP$, inside the
ordinary range, though the conventional $t$-test would have reported them as significant. The
earliest event, the March 2020 state of emergency, falls inside the first 60 sessions and
cannot be estimated; it is the largest single move in the sample, so the exclusion works
against the result rather than for it.

Read one event at a time, that is a thin result. Read in aggregate, the effect is there.
Per-event tests suffer from multiple comparisons, and with \NTested{} events at a 5\% threshold
roughly one would clear by chance. The aggregate test asks the question directly, whether mean
absolute movement is larger on event days. Across the \AggN{} distinct event sessions, mean
$|CAR|$ is $\EvMeanAbs\%$ against $\NullMeanAbs\%$ on sessions without an event. Compared
through $\AggPerm$ random draws from the null, the difference has $p = \AggP$. $\AggObs$ events
pass the 95th percentile against $\AggExp$ expected from chance alone. Political news produces
larger movements than background noise, and the error margin on any single event is too wide
to separate it from noise.

from its possibly landing in an unusual regime. Table~\ref{tab:window} re-runs the complete

The conclusion does not depend on the window, but the fine detail does. The lowest rank
correlation across the four windows is $\RhoWindow$, so the ordering is close to but not
exactly stable. The count of events at $p < 0.05$ rises from $\NPfiveWshort$ on the shortest
window to $\NPfiveWlong$ on the longest, driven by the null's 95th percentile, which moves from
$\PqWshort\%$ to $\PqWlong\%$. A reader who wanted the shortest count could take the
shortest window and a reader who wanted six could take the longest. That the two ends of the
range both include the May 2025 run-off, the court annulment and the Fitch revision is the
robust part; the exact count is a property of the calibration rather than of the data.
Restricting the calculation to the post-COVID regime gives a 95th percentile of
$\PqPostCovid\%$, so the fat tails are not an artifact of the pandemic.

One confound belongs here rather than in a footnote. The longest window needs 250 sessions of
history, so its null is built from sessions that all postdate the 2020 crash, and its 95th
percentile is therefore lower partly because the sample excludes the volatility of the
pandemic and not only because the window is longer. The four percentiles above are not
comparable to each other with total precision, and we would rather say so than attribute the
whole movement to the window.

\subsection{Group statistics, reported as description}

Mean CAR$[-1,+1]$ across domestic news with an expected negative sign is $\NegMean\%$ over
$\NegN$ events, and $\TrioMean\%$ for the 2024--2025 electoral trio. We report both as
description of the class rather than as inference, because the expected-sign labels were
assigned by the researcher with knowledge of the outcomes, so any test built on that
classification is circular by construction.

Anticipated events do not move the market. The 2021 no-confidence motion that ousted the
C\^{\i}È›u government with 281 votes, the largest such vote in the country's history, leaves a
CAR of $-0.19\%$. The December 2020 parliamentary elections, in which AUR surprised, leave
$-0.18\%$. In both cases the outcome was public before voting day.

\section{How long does the recovery take?}

The obvious next question is whether the damage is temporary, and the market does come back.
That observation is not evidence of anything, for the same reason the placebo test was needed
above: random drops of the same depth also come back.

For each political drop of at least 1\% we count the trading days until the close returns to
its pre-shock level. Each event is compared with a matched control sample of random drops
whose depth falls within a tolerance of that event's own depth, so the comparison does not
depend on how depths are distributed across events. The control pool excludes sessions within
three days of any real political event and no drop is drawn twice, so the controls are
independent of the events they are meant to contrast with.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Recovery after political shocks of at least 1\%.}
\label{tab:recovery}

Recovery is universal, and it is equally universal for drops with no political news attached.
All \RecPolRecovered\% of political drops of at least 1\% return to the pre-shock close within
60 sessions, and \RecPlRecovered\% of the matched random drops do the same. On time to recovery
the paired difference is $\RecMedDiff$ sessions in favour of the control sample, so political
shocks recover if anything more slowly. Across the $\RecPairN$ usable pairs they recover
faster in $\RecFasterN$, and the permutation test gives no support for faster recovery. With
$\RecPairN$ pairs this test has almost no power, which is itself the point: the sample is too
small to distinguish the two distributions.

The reason is the sample period. BET-TR rose substantially over 2020--2026, which makes
``back to where it was'' a low bar, and a low bar says nothing about panic. The two shocks
with the longest recovery are the two where the market kept falling afterwards, which is
precisely when buying the dip would have hurt.

\section{Is the effect tradable?}

\subsection{Little is left after the news is public}

The opening gap absorbs between $\GapShareLo$\% and $\GapShareHi$\% of the three-day
cumulative abnormal return around each major shock. What remains for someone who reacts to the
news, after it is published and the price has already moved, is between $\AfterNewsMin$\% and
$\AfterNewsMax$\% in absolute terms. A rule that reacts to news trades the remainder. A rule
that would profit has to hold a position from before the event, which is a forecasting
problem, and this study contains no evidence about it, since election outcomes are settled
after the vote.

\subsection{The gross edge is smaller than the cost of trading}

We declared twelve candidate rules in advance and split the sample chronologically into
\ReguliTrain\ train and \ReguliTest\ test events. One of the twelve is an overlay that takes no
position at all, so the max statistic that pays for the search is taken over the remaining
\NTradable{} rules. The best rule ($\BestRule$) earns $\BestGross\%$ per trade gross and
$\BestNetFifty$\% net at 50 basis points.

Breakeven sits at $\BreakevenBps$ basis points, interpolated between the two cost levels that
straddle the crossing. Commission, spread and market impact on a market this illiquid plausibly
run at 50 to 200 basis points in each direction, and we did not measure them; if that range is
wrong the verdict changes, which is why the verdict rests on the cost table rather than on any
single number. The chance of finding something that looks significant by luck alone across
\NTradable{} attempts is \LuckChance\%.

The reality check on the max statistic returns $p = \RealityP$, and we report it with a caveat
that matters more than the number. The best observed $t$ across the tradable rules is
negative, so every null draw beats it by construction and the test has no power to reject
anything. What it does not say is that the rules are indistinguishable from noise; it says
that no rule has a positive expected return after costs, which the cost table already
establishes directly. A reality check earns its keep when some rule looks good, and that is
exactly the case that does not arise here.

\subsection{The constraint is the sample, not the search}

With $n = \NPower$ events, a per-trade standard deviation of $\SdTrade\%$ and a standard error
of $\SeTrade\%$, the minimum detectable effect at 5\% significance and 50\% power is $\Mde\%$ per
trade. Reaching 80\% power at that effect would need roughly $\NNeed$ events, about nine times
what the period contains. A Romanian electoral cycle offers two or three rounds a year and the
useful measurement window is one to three sessions, so this design yields tens of events, not
hundreds.

An effect of this size would therefore not be visible at this sample size, whether or not it
exists. More rules cannot fix that. Only more events can.

\subsection{One loose thread}

The rule entering at the open of the event day outperformed the rule entering a session
earlier, and the two differ only in the session before the event. That implies the average
pre-event session was slightly positive, of order 0.2\%, which would be a small anticipatory
drift rather than a news-trading effect. At 24 events this is indistinguishable from noise and
we leave it unresolved. It is the one finding here that a prospective register could test and
a retrospective sample cannot.

\section{Three results that turned out to be measurement failures}\label{sec:masurari}

The three most striking results of the analysis are not market phenomena.

\subsection{The CiucÄƒ government: a global selloff day}

The 25 November 2021 event shows a CAR of $\CiucaCar\%$ with $t = \CiucaT$, among the largest
in the table. The raw path shows otherwise. 26 November 2021 was the global selloff triggered
by the designation of the Omicron variant, with STOXX 600 at $\CiucaStoxx\%$ and BET at
$\CiucaBet\%$, closing at the low of the day. BET fell less than Europe. The investiture day
itself was positive, at $\CiucaInvestDay\%$. Volumes confirm a market-wide move: TLV at 4.09
million shares against roughly 1.6 million previously, BRD at 175,000 against about 69,000,
SNG at 180,000 against about 14,000.

The $-2.04\%$ residual reported as a STOXX-adjusted effect is an artifact of beta instability.
The beta the model estimates on its own 50-session window is $\CiucaBetaEst$; re-estimated on the
50 sessions after the event it is $\CiucaBetaPost$, and on the shock session itself the two
indices moved almost one for one, BET at $\CiucaBet\%$ against STOXX at $\CiucaStoxx\%$. The
model therefore predicts a small BET decline, BET falls almost in line with Europe, and the
model books a fictitious domestic shortfall. A market control is not conservative by
construction. Any check of an effect's domestic origin has to compare raw returns with STOXX,
not only the model residual. The explanation we initially entertained, that the market taxed
the grand coalition, has no support: such a tax would have appeared on the investiture day,
which was positive.

\subsection{June 2024 elections: dividend season}

The event shows $\LocaleBet\%$ with $t = \LocaleT$. On total returns the same event is
$\LocaleTr\%$. The cause is the banks' ex-dividend season, which falls exactly in the event
window. The pipeline flags this type of contamination automatically and flags no other event
in the series. Unlike the case above, this is a limitation we could act on: the event is
excluded from the group test, and doing so changes nothing that follows. It remains in the
per-event and aggregate sets, where its $p$-value is large and it changes nothing there either.

\subsection{The court annulment: a rebound that had already started}

The Constitutional Court's decision of 6 December 2024 shows $+\CcrArZero\%$ on day 0 and
$\CcrCar\%$ over three sessions, with one of the largest $t$-statistics in the table. The
rebound had begun on 4 December, two sessions before the decision: BET traded $-3.24\%$
intraday, then reversed violently and closed green. The estimation window ends around
19 November and does not contain the late-November decline, so a stale baseline reads the
post-crash rebound as a positive abnormal return. Unlike the dividend case we cannot drop
this event without changing the headline result, so we keep it in every set and flag it. It
is one of the three events that clear the threshold, and its $p = \CcrP$ should be read as
conditional on a baseline that we can show to be stale.

\section{Limitations}

There are \NEvents{} manually dated events, and the calibration above puts the detection
threshold at of order 4\% over three sessions, so this design cannot settle many further
questions about such effects. The number of events reaching 5\% varies between $\NPfiveWshort$
and $\NPfiveWlong$ with the estimation window, though the same three clear it at every window.
We report the range rather than pick a window. No official total-return series exist for the
individual stocks, so
BET-TR covers the index only and dividend contamination remains possible at the stock level.
BET's composition changes over time and the basket used here is fixed. RSS covers only the
present, so the historical base depends on manual dating, and two events carry medium
confidence. BVB is illiquid, so the intraday inference rests on high-turnover sessions.

External validation is partial and we state the limits. The daily data reproduce the reported
closes: BET at 16,982 points and $-0.89\%$ on 25 November 2024 \cite{bursa}, and $-0.45\%$
where the press reports ``about half a percent'' on the second day in May 2025 \cite{profit}.
The intraday figure of $-1.69\%$ \cite{profit} cannot be checked against our data, because the
feed returns closing prices per 15-minute bar rather than intrabar extremes; our lowest bar
that day is $-2.88\%$, so the reported low is not contradicted but not confirmed either.

\section{Conclusion}

Romanian politics moves BVB fast, and the market does recover from the resulting drops. But
recovery is not a reason to buy, since random drops of the same depth recover in slightly less
time, and the gross edge is smaller than the cost of trading. The effect is real in aggregate
and unexploitable in practice, which is the honest description of a market this small and this
illiquid rather than a failure of the study.

One methodological failure is worth recording for anyone repeating this work, because it cut in
the flattering direction. Costs were originally subtracted with the sign of the position, so a
short was credited the transaction cost instead of paying it and short returns rose as costs
rose. The same rule that looked like a thin positive margin net of 50 basis points loses money
once the sign is corrected. A cost error of this kind is invisible in the gross numbers and
surfaces only when the cost parameter is varied.

What the sample cannot do is settle the question. The remedy is a prospective register: fix a
rule, record each shock together with the news that arrived with it, and fill in the outcome
only afterwards. The accompanying code implements one, writing each detection to a log before
the outcome is known, so that a few years of accumulation could settle what 24 retrospective
events cannot. The code, the data, the placebo test and the figures are available for
replication and extension.
