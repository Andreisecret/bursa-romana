\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Question.} How fast, and how strongly, do shares on the Bucharest Stock
Exchange react to political news? We apply an event study to \NEvents{} events over
2020--2026, using daily quotes for the BET index, 8 liquid stocks and 4 complementary
indices, plus 15-minute bars around the three major electoral shocks.

\noindent\textbf{Speed.} The response to electoral shocks forms at the open of the first
session after the event. In November 2024 the intraday low came 15 minutes after the open.

\noindent\textbf{Magnitude, in two senses.} Results are calibrated with a placebo test of 500
pseudo-events run through an identical estimator. The null distribution of three-day
cumulative abnormal returns has $\sigma = \NullSd\%$ and a 95th percentile of
$\NullPqfive\%$, so a 3\% move over three days occurs on roughly $\NullFreqThree\%$ of BVB
sessions with no political news at all. Read one event at a time, three of the \NEvents{}
events clear the 5\% threshold, the second-round presidential victory being the clearest at
$\TurTwoCar\%$ and $p = \TurTwoP$. Read in aggregate, political events do depart significantly
from ordinary noise: mean absolute movement on event days is $\EvMeanAbs\%$ against
$\NullMeanAbs\%$ on ordinary days, $p = \AggP$ in a permutation test, with $\AggObs$ events
above the 95th percentile against $\AggExp$ expected by chance alone.

\noindent\textbf{Robustness.} Re-running the placebo test with four estimation windows, from
40 to 250 sessions, leaves the ranking of events unchanged (rank correlation
$\RhoWindow$). Restricting the null to the post-COVID regime gives a 95th percentile of
$\PqPostCovid\%$, essentially the full-period value, so the fat tails are not an artifact of
the pandemic.

\noindent\textbf{Cross-section.} The large electoral shocks are domestic: STOXX Europe 600
was flat or slightly positive on the event days while BET diverged sharply. Banks absorb the
first blow, with TLV at $\TlvMay\%$ in a single session.

\noindent\emph{Secondary contribution, methodological.} Three of the most eye-catching results
of an ordinary event study turn out to be measurement failures rather than market phenomena.
A fixed estimation window that lands in a calm regime inflates every $t$-statistic at once.
The public BVB feed's adjustment flag does not correct for dividends, so ex-dividend windows
are contaminated. A market control estimated in calm conditions can turn a global crash day
into a fictitious domestic effect. Each of the three produced results in this study that,
taken at face value, would have supported wrong conclusions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Data and method}

\subsection{Quotes}

The Bucharest Stock Exchange publishes no developer API, but its website runs a
TradingView-compatible datafeed at \url{https://wapi.bvb.ro/api} that answers plain HTTP
requests. The parameters are \texttt{symbol}, \texttt{from}, \texttt{to}, \texttt{rs},
\texttt{ajust}, \texttt{countback} and \texttt{currencyCode}, and the daily resolution must be
requested as \texttt{rs=1D}. We downloaded daily bars in RON for 1 January 2020 to
28 September 2026: the BET index, 8 liquid stocks (TLV, SNP, BRD, H2O, SNG, DIGI, TEL, SNN)
and 4 complementary indices (ROTX, BET-TR, BET-FI, BET-NG), about 1,690 sessions per symbol.
H2O enters the series only in July 2023, with the largest IPO in the exchange's history. For
the speed analysis we also downloaded 15-minute bars for BET and TLV around the three major
shocks.

One limitation of the source changes the results, so we state it here. The \texttt{ajust} flag
corrects stock splits but not dividends, and the check is direct: TLV's return on 11 June
2024, $-4.69\%$, is identical in the adjusted and unadjusted series. Price returns are
therefore contaminated in ex-dividend windows, and the Romanian banks' dividend season, in
June, falls in the middle of the window we analyse. The primary series is accordingly
\textbf{BET-TR}, the exchange's total-return index, which is immune to this effect. In
parallel, the pipeline flags any event where the gap between BET and BET-TR exceeds one
percentage point over the same window.

\subsection{Benchmark and events}

As a model of the European market we used STOXX Europe 600, downloaded daily from Yahoo
Finance's public chart interface and aligned to the exchange's calendar by forward-filling the
last available value.

Articles from the RSS feeds of G4Media, HotNews, Digi24 and Economica were collected
automatically and keyword-filtered for political content. RSS covers only the present, so the
historical base is a table of \NEvents{} manually dated events, each with a confidence level
and an \emph{ex-ante} classification of origin (domestic, external, market) and expected
direction. Table~\ref{tab:events} lists them.

\subsection{Estimator}

The estimation window is $[-60,-11]$ trading days, truncated at the start of the series, with
a minimum of 30 observations. Event windows are $[0,0]$, $[-1,+1]$ and $[-5,+5]$. For BET and
the indices the abnormal return is $AR_t = R_t - \bar{R}_{est}$. For the individual stocks we
use an OLS market model against BET, and for the external control an OLS model of BET against
STOXX 600, both estimated over the estimation window. The conventional statistic is
$t = CAR / (\hat{\sigma}\sqrt{T})$.

Day 0 is the session in which the news appears if that falls before 18:00, and the next
trading session otherwise. Every ballot was held on a Sunday evening, so day 0 is the Monday
and the opening price carries the full weekend information.

\subsection{Placebo calibration}

The $t$-statistics above assume normally distributed, independent returns with volatility
measured over the estimation window. On a small and illiquid market that assumption is
problematic: a fixed window of 50 sessions can land in a calm regime and understate the
volatility used as the denominator. To remove the dependence on a parameter we chose
ourselves, we calibrate with a placebo test. We draw 500 pseudo-random trading sessions,
excluding three sessions either side of every real event, and apply the identical estimator.
The resulting distribution shows how large an ordinary CAR is at BVB, and each real event
receives a two-sided empirical $p$-value on $|CAR|$.

Two further tests follow from the same null. The aggregate test asks whether mean absolute
movement is larger on event days than on ordinary days, which sidesteps the multiple-comparisons
problem of examining 24 events individually. The window-sensitivity test re-runs the whole
calibration under four different estimation windows, which addresses the paper's own objection
that a fixed window may be unrepresentative.

\subsection{The large electoral shocks are domestic}

On the key days STOXX Europe 600 is close to flat: $+0.06\%$ on 25 November 2024, $+0.16\%$

comparison rather than on the model residual, for reasons given in

\subsection{Placebo calibration}

The placebo test produces the most consequential result in the paper, because it recalibrates
the reading of everything else. The null distribution of CAR$[-1,+1]$ has a mean of $-0.03\%$
and $\sigma = \NullSd\%$, with $|CAR_3|$ at the 95th percentile of $\NullPqfive\%$ and a
maximum of $\NullMax\%$. At BVB a 3\% move over three sessions occurs in $\NullFreqThree\%$ of
sessions with no political news, so the effective detectability threshold for a political
event is of order 4\%.

Of \NEvents{} political events over six and a half years, three clear the 5\% threshold: the
second-round presidential victory in May 2025 at $\TurTwoCar\%$ and $p = \TurTwoP$
(percentile $\TurTwoPct$), the Constitutional Court's annulment of the election at
$\CcrCar\%$ and $p = \CcrP$, and the Fitch outlook revision at $-\FitchCar\%$ and
$p = \FitchP$. The December 2024 parliamentary elections sit at $p = \ParlP$, inside the
ordinary range, though the conventional $t$-test would have reported them as significant.

\subsection{The aggregate test: do political news move the market overall?}

Per-event tests suffer from multiple comparisons: with \NEvents{} events and a 5\% threshold,
roughly one would clear by chance. The aggregate test avoids the problem by asking the
question directly, namely whether mean absolute movement is larger on event days.

Across the \AggN{} distinct event sessions, mean $|CAR|$ is $\EvMeanAbs\%$, against
$\NullMeanAbs\%$ on sessions without an event. We compared the two through \AggPerm{} random
draws from the null, and the difference has $p = \AggP$. Three events pass the 95th percentile
against $\AggExp$ expected from chance alone. The conclusion, in the form that survives these
tests, is that political news produces larger movements at BVB than background noise, while
the error margin on any single event is too wide to separate it from noise.

The set of events passing the threshold does not change at all, and the rank correlation across
windows is $\RhoWindow$, so the conclusion does not depend on the window. Two details still
deserve mention. The number of events reaching 5\% rises from three to four as the window
lengthens, because the null's 95th percentile falls from $\NullPqfive\%$ to roughly 3.4\%.
And restricting the calculation to the post-COVID regime, excluding the 2020 and 2021
restrictions, gives a 95th percentile of $\PqPostCovid\%$, practically identical to the
full-period value: the fat tails of the distribution are not an artifact of the pandemic.

\subsection{Group statistics, reported as description}

Mean CAR$[-1,+1]$ across domestic news with an expected negative sign is $\NegMean\%$ over
$\NegN$ events, and $\TrioMean\%$ for the 2024--2025 electoral trio. We report both as
description of the class rather than as inference. The expected-sign labels were assigned by
the researcher with knowledge of the outcomes, so any test built on that classification is
circular by construction. A legitimate test would require a prospective register of events,
announced before they occur.

Anticipated events do not move the market. The 2021 no-confidence motion that ousted the
C\^{\i}țu government with 281 votes, the largest such vote in the country's history, leaves a
CAR of $-0.19\%$. The December 2020 parliamentary elections, in which AUR surprised, leave
$-0.18\%$. In both cases the outcome was public before voting day.

\section{Three results that turned out to be measurement failures}\label{sec:masurari}

The three most striking results of the analysis are not market phenomena.

\subsection{The Ciucă government: a global selloff day}

The 25 November 2021 event shows a CAR of $\CiucaCar\%$ with $t = \CiucaT$, among the largest
in the table. The raw path shows otherwise. 26 November 2021 was the global selloff triggered
by the designation of the Omicron variant, with STOXX 600 at $\CiucaStoxx\%$ and BET at
$\CiucaBet\%$, closing at the low of the day. BET fell less than Europe. The investiture day
itself was positive, at $\CiucaInvestDay\%$. Volumes confirm a market-wide move: TLV at 4.09
million shares against roughly 1.6 million previously, BRD at 175,000 against about 69,000,
SNG at 180,000 against about 14,000.

The $-2.04\%$ residual reported as a STOXX-adjusted effect is an artifact of beta instability.
The beta estimated over the calm window, about 0.37, understates the crisis co-movement, about
0.93, so the model predicts a small BET decline, BET falls almost in line with Europe, and the
model books a fictitious domestic shortfall. A market control is not conservative by
construction. Any check of an effect's domestic origin has to compare raw returns with STOXX,
not only the model residual. The explanation we initially entertained, that the market taxed
the grand coalition, has no support: such a tax would have appeared on the investiture day,
which was positive.

\subsection{June 2024 elections: dividend season}

The event shows $-3.01\%$ with $t = \LocaleT$. On total returns the same event is
$\LocaleTr\%$. The cause is the banks' ex-dividend season, which falls exactly in the event
window. The pipeline now flags this type of contamination automatically, and flags no other
event in the series.

\subsection{The court annulment: a rebound that had already started}

The Constitutional Court's decision of 6 December 2024 shows $+2.97\%$ on day 0 and
$\CcrCar\%$ over three sessions, with one of the largest $t$-statistics in the table. The
rebound had begun on 4 December, two sessions before the decision: BET traded $-3.24\%$
intraday, then reversed violently and closed green. The estimation window ends around
19 November and does not contain the late-November decline, so a stale baseline reads the
post-crash rebound as a positive abnormal return. We keep the movement as an observation,
since STOXX was flat and the domestic divergence is real, but exclude it from inference.

\section{Limitations}

Statistical power is low by construction. There are \NEvents{} manually dated events, and the
placebo calibration shows the detection threshold at BVB is of order 4\% over three sessions,
so this design cannot settle many further questions about such effects. The number of events
reaching the 5\% threshold varies with the estimation window, between three and four, even
though their identity does not; we report this rather than pick a window. No official
total-return series exist for the individual stocks, so BET-TR covers the index only and
dividend contamination remains possible at the stock level. BET's composition changes over
time and the basket used here is fixed. RSS covers only the present, so the historical base
depends on manual dating, and two events carry medium confidence. BVB is illiquid, so the
intraday inference rests on high-turnover sessions.

External validation is exact: the data reproduce the figures reported in the financial press,
with BET at 16,982 points and $-0.89\%$ on 25 November 2024 \cite{bursa}, $-1.69\%$ intraday
and $-0.5\%$ on the second day in May 2025 \cite{profit}, and all indices red \cite{gandul,digi}.

\section{Conclusion}

Romanian politics moves BVB fast. The Monday opening price is the reaction, and in November
2024 the intraday low came 15 minutes after the open. The cross-section is coherent: banks
absorb the first blow, energy damps, and the large electoral shocks are demonstrably domestic.

On magnitude the answer has two halves, and the first is the important one. In aggregate,
political news produces movements larger than background noise, a gap of $\EvMeanAbs\%$
against $\NullMeanAbs\%$ in mean absolute movement at $p = \AggP$. Taken one at a time, only
three of \NEvents{} events reach 5\%, and one of those three turns out to owe more to something
else than to politics. Event studies on illiquid markets seldom report more, and the distance
between a publishable result and a wrong one lies in the test that cannot be fooled by
multiple comparisons.

For an investor the practical conclusion is unchanged: reaction windows open at 10:00 and
close within minutes. What changes is the reading. Not every piece of electoral news deserves
a position, only moves of whole percentage points, and the return belongs to the gap between
the expected and the received.

The methodological contribution is probably the most transferable. A fixed estimation window
can land in a calm regime and inflate every $t$-statistic at once; here the effect showed up
as a difference between three and four significant events depending on the window. A price
adjustment that covers splits but not dividends can manufacture anomalies of two or three
percentage points in the middle of dividend season. A market control calibrated in calm
conditions can turn a global crash day into a fictitious domestic effect. All three produced
results in this study that, taken at face value, would have supported wrong conclusions. The
code, the data, the placebo test and the figures are available for replication and extension.
